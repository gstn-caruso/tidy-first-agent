# 11. Chunk Statements

*Tidy First?*, cap. 11, p. 23.

## Disparador y movimiento

> This one wins the prize for simplest tidying. You're reading a big chunk of code and you realize, "Oh, this part does this and then that part does that." Put a blank line between the parts.

## Por qué importa algo tan chico

> I like that this tidying is so, so simple. That's part of the philosophy of Tidy First?—don't make software design such a big deal that you are in danger of not doing it.
>
> Software design enables change. A little software design can make change a little easier.
>
> Here's the cool thing—compound interest. Software design also makes more software design easier. This is both a blessing and a curse. You can get caught in the vortex of design and forget to make the change. Don't do that.

## Encadena con (caps. 11 y 17)

> After you've chunked statements, you have many paths forward, such as Explaining Variables (Chapter 8), Extract Helper (Chapter 12), or Explaining Comments (Chapter 14).
>
> You can precede each chunk with an explaining comment. You may extract a chunk as an explaining helper.
